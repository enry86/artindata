# Post text · The cracking Earth

## Instagram caption
Every earthquake of magnitude 4.5 or more in 2025: 8,556 of them, each drawn as
a ray of light leaving the globe at its epicentre.

Ray length is magnitude. Colour is depth, from gold at the surface to deep red
below 300 km. Where quakes sit close together, thin lines join them, and the
plate boundaries open up like cracks.

The tallest ray is the M8.8 off Kamchatka on 29 July, the largest earthquake
since 2011. One in five of the year's quakes struck that single region, mostly
its aftershocks.

Data: USGS Earthquake Catalog (public domain). The lines are a proximity
rule, not faults, and half the depths are the catalog's 10 km default. Full
method and code: link in bio.

## Alt text
A dark globe against a starfield, seen over Indonesia and Australia, with faint
continents. Glowing red-orange seams run along the plate boundaries: the arc of
Sumatra and Java, the Philippines and the Kermadec-Tonga trench. Thousands of
thin rays of light stand out from the surface at each earthquake epicentre. The
rays on the far side rise past the rim like a corona. Ray length is magnitude and
colour is depth, from pale gold at the surface to deep red. The longest ray, at
the upper right, is the magnitude 8.8 Kamchatka earthquake of 29 July 2025,
labelled in the picture. A key at lower left shows ray lengths for magnitudes 5
to 8 and the depth scale from 0 to 700 kilometres.
