# Post text · The restless Sun

## Instagram caption
278 years of sunspots, one disc per year, from 1749 to 2026.

Each disc is the Sun in that year, with one dark spot for every 10 units of the
sunspot number. Busy years are pitted and bright; quiet years are clean and dim.
The big discs are 1957, the busiest year on record (27 spots), 2025, the last full
year (12), and 1810, when the Sun was spotless. The grid below is every year in
order, 20 per row, and the bands running through it are the solar cycle, about
11 years from calm to storm and back. The long clean stretch around 1810 is the
Dalton minimum.

"Spots" here stand for sunspot-number units, not real sunspots: the number is
10 × groups + spots, calibrated. Where each spot sits on its disc is random.

Data: SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium (CC BY-NC 4.0). 2026 covers January to
September. Full method and code: link in bio.

## Alt text
A dark, warm-black scene glowing orange. Three suns sit in a row at the top. The
large one in the middle, labelled 1957, the busiest year, is bright white-gold at
the centre with 27 dark spots ringed in brown. The small one on the left, 2025, has
12 spots. The small one on the right, 1810, is a dim, plain orange disc with no
spots. Below them, a grid of 278 small suns, 20 per row, runs from 1749 at the top
left to 2026 at the bottom right. Busy years are bright, haloed and dotted with
dark spots; quiet years are dim, matte and clean. The busy and quiet years form
diagonal bands that repeat about every 11 years, with a long clean stretch near the
top left around 1810. Three discs in the grid are ringed in pale gold: 1810, 1957
and 2025. A key reads "1 spot = 10 on the sunspot number".
